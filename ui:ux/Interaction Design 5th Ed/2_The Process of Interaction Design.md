# 2 - The Process of Interaction Design

## 2.1

- "Double diamond" of design with 4 phases:
  - Discover: Gather insights about problem
  - Define: Develop a clear brief framing the design challenge
  - Develop: sollutions or concepts are created, iterated, tested
  - Deliver: Project finalized, produced, launched
- Philosophy of user-centered design: involve users through developed

## 2.2 What is Involved in Interaction Design?

![alt text](<CleanShot 2026-07-22 at 13.25.36@2x.png>)

- Artifact's use and domain investiggated by taking a user-centered approach to development.
- Opinions/reactions to designs are sought
- Users involved in the development process
- Design about tradeofs: Example: How much choice?
- Generating alternatives: key principle. "The best way to get a good idea is to get lots of ideas"
- Involve others in the design process - capture design in a form allowing review, revision and improvement
- Sketches, diagrams, series of natural language descriptions
- Understanding the problem space
  - Make design decisions after articulating nature of the problem space:
    - What is the current UX of product, why a change is needed, how will changing improve user experience?
- Involving users in development: Product owner (business and technical knowledge)
  - Expectation management: Make sure user's expectations of product are realistic
    - No surprises when product arrives
  - Ownership: Users who are involved and have contributed feel a sense of ownership and support its use
- Degrees of User Involvement
  - Participatory design: End users are active participants of design process
- What is a user centered approach?
  - More of a philosophy:
    - Early focus on users/tasks:
      - Users goals are driving force behind dev
      - Behavior and context of use is studied, system is designed to support them
      - Characteristics are captrued and designed for
      - Users are consulted throughout development
      - Design decisions are taken within the context of their users, activities, environment.
    - Empirical measurement: Usability and UX Goals should be ID'd, clearly documented, and agreed upon
    - Iterative design
      - Designs refined based on feedback

## 2.3 Some Practical Users

- Who are users?
  - Wide collection of people who have stake in development of a successful product: Stakeholders
  - Onion diagram to model stakeholders: Concentric circles of stakeholder zones
- What are the user needs?
  - People don't necessarily know what is possible
  - Explore problem space, investigating users and activities, trying out ideas with potential users to see whether ideasare successful
- How to generate alternative designs
  - Discuss ideas with other designers, browse a collection of designs
  - Compare different fields to each other - new ones, transferring properties between domains
- How to Choose among alternative designs
  - Externally visible and internal factors
    - Enable stakeholder interaction: Designs need to be in a form that can be evaluated by others
    - Process of writing down formal, verifiable usability criteria: Usability engineering
