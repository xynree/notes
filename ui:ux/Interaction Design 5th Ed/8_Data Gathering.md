# 8 - Data Gathering

# 8.2 - Five Key Issues

- Setting Goals
  - Goals influence nature of gathering sessions and techniques to be used
- Identifying Partipants
  - Study population
- Relationship with Participants
  - Make sure relationship is clear and professional
  - Informed consent form
  - Incentives may be needed
- Triangulation
  - Investigation of a phenomena from at least 2 different perspectives
    - Triangulation of data: drawn from different sources at different times, in different places, or from different people
    - Investigator triangulation: Different people involved in collecting and interpreting data
    - Theory triangulation: Different theoretical frameworks to view data or findings
    - Methodological triangulation: Different data gathering techniques
- Pilot studies
  - Trial run of main study: Make sure proposed method is viable

# 8.4 Interviews

- "Conversation with a purpose"
- 4 types of interviews: Open ended/unstructured, structured, semi-strucutred, group interviews
- Approach to interview depends on purpose and goal
- Unstructured Interviews
  - Open questions: No expectation about format or content of the answers
  - Interviewee free to answer as fully or briefly as they want
  - Should still have a plan of the main topics to be covered so they can make sure topics are discussed
  - Generates rich data that is complex - deep understanding, rich insights
- Structured Interviews
  - Intervewer asks predetermined qustions similar to a questionairre
  - Short and clearly qorded questions with closed answers (require an answer from a predetermined set of options)
  - Closed questions work well if range of possible answer is known or if participants don't have much time
- Semi-Structured interviews
  - Interviewer has basic script for guidance, starting with preplanned questions, then probes the interviewer to say more
  - Important to not pre-empt an answer
  - Probes can be useful "Do you wnat to tell me anything else"
- Planning or conducting an interviews
  - Develop sets of questions/topics to be covered
  - Developing open-ended or close-ended questions
  - Split up long or compound questions into separate questions
  - Explain in a straightforward way
  - Keep questions neutral

# 8.5 - Questionnaires

- Questionnaires can be distributed to large number of participants
- Ensure questions are clearly worded and data collected can be analyzed efficiently
- Motivation high enough to complete questionnaire w/o anyone present - questionnaire appropriate
- Questions should be specific, with close ended answers including a "no opinion" or "none of these"

- Questionnaire Structure
  - Basic demographics, details of relevant experience
  - Think of ordering of questions
  - Are differnet versions needed for different populations?
  - Think about length, allow responders to opt out at difffernt stages
  - Question/Response formats
    - Checkboxes/ranges
    - Rating scales
    - Likert Scales
    - Semantic Difference Scales
- difficult to get random sample of respondents in online questionnaires
- Make sure to plan survey timeline

# 8.6 - Observation

- Data gathering for observation should have a clearly stated goal
- One framework:
  - The person, the place, the tihing
- Another (focusing on context of activity):
  - Space, Actors, Activities, OBjects, Acts, Events, Time, Goal, Feelings
- Direct observation in controlled environments
  - "Think aloud"
- Indirect Observation: Tracking user activities
  - Diaries
  - Logged data from analytics
